BLOCKER: D13 (1N4148W) is oriented with the cathode on +5V and anode on LINE_MUTE_K. The relay coil (K1) is driven by Q2 (2N7002) with source at GND. When Q2 turns off, the inductive kick drives the LINE_MUTE_K node positive. The diode as placed blocks this positive spike from reaching +5V, leaving the gate of Q2 unprotected against a voltage far exceeding its 60V rating. The diode must be reversed (cathode to LINE_MUTE_K, anode to +5V) to clamp the flyback.

BLOCKER: D2 (SMBJ12A) is a 12V bidirectional TVS diode connected between VBOOST_IN and GND. The net name VBOOST_IN implies it feeds a boost converter input, which typically requires a voltage higher than the source (e.g., >5V or >9V). If VBOOST_IN exceeds 12V, the TVS will conduct heavily, causing thermal runaway and destruction. Verify the maximum voltage on VBOOST_IN; if it exceeds 12V, this part is wrong.

SHOULD FIX: D15, D16, D17 are 1N4148W diodes used to OR-logic KEEP_ON, VBUS_FUSED, and +9V_FUSED into BOOST_EN. The 1N4148W has a forward voltage drop of ~0.7V. If BOOST_EN is a digital input on a boost controller requiring a specific high threshold (e.g., 2.5V or 3.3V), the 0.7V drop may prevent the signal from registering as "high" when the source is only 5V (5V - 0.7V = 4.3V, which is usually fine, but if the source is 3.3V, 3.3V - 0.7V = 2.6V, which is marginal). More critically, if the boost controller's BOOST_EN pin has an internal pull-down or requires a specific voltage level, the diode drop may cause issues. However, the bigger risk is that 1N4148W is a small signal diode; if the boost controller draws significant current on BOOST_EN, the diode may overheat. Verify the current draw on BOOST_EN and the required high-level voltage.

SHOULD FIX: D24 (BAT54) is a Schottky diode with a forward voltage drop of ~0.3-0.4V. It is placed between BIAS5 (5V) and BIAS_SRC. If BIAS_SRC is intended to be a 5V mic bias, the actual voltage will be ~4.6-4.7V. If the downstream circuit requires a precise 5V bias, this drop may be unacceptable. Verify the required bias voltage.

NOTE: D11 (USBLC6-2SC6) is a USB ESD array. The pinout listed is 1=USB_DP, 2=GND, 3=USB_DM, 4=USB_DM, 5=VBUS, 6=USB_DP. This is a 6-pin SOT-23-6 package. The standard USBLC6-2SC6 pinout is 1=DP, 2=GND, 3=DM, 4=DM, 5=VBUS, 6=DP. This matches. However, ensure that the USB_DP and USB_DM nets are correctly assigned to the differential pair and that VBUS is connected to the 5V rail.

NOTE: D4 and D5 are LEDs. D4 (blue) is connected with cathode to GND and anode to PWR_LED_A. D5 (red) is connected with cathode to GND and anode to REC_LED_A. This is correct for common-cathode LEDs. Ensure that PWR_LED_A and REC_LED_A are driven with sufficient current (typically 5-20mA) and that the driving circuit includes a current-limiting resistor.

NOTE: D1, D10, D14 are B340A Schottky diodes used for OR-ing power sources into VBOOST_IN. The B340A has a forward voltage drop of ~0.4-0.5V. This is acceptable for power OR-ing. Ensure that the diodes are rated for the maximum current expected on VBOOST_IN.

NOTE: D18 and D19 are 1N4148W diodes used to isolate REC_SW and GAIN_SW from the Teensy inputs. This is a common technique to prevent back-feed. Ensure that the diode drop does not affect the logic levels if the Teensy inputs are sensitive to voltage levels.

NOTE: D6-D9 and D20-D23 are PESD12VL1BA ESD diodes connected from RAW/RAWC inputs to CHASSIS. This is correct for ESD protection. Ensure that CHASSIS is connected to the ground plane and that the diodes are placed close to the input connectors.
